## What this topic is

qq is modelled on Chromium's infrastructure (depot_tools, gclient, recipes,
LUCI, Sheriff-o-Matic, AutoRoll and release channels), rebuilt for small
GitHub repos. Its parts live in 13 repos under
[quirq-ai](https://github.com/quirq-ai), and two product repos, innernet
(Next.js) and xo-space (Python), use it; a third, website (Gatsby), is being
onboarded. Reading 13 READMEs is a slow way to
learn how the parts connect, so this topic maps them in one interactive app,
with a page for each repo. The same app carries the checklist for the qq
alpha, where 10 to 15 people use qq for their real work and say what hurt. Alongside the app,
a one-pager, a slide deck and six reports cover qq's phases (v0, v1 and v2),
the v0 status, how to set up qq and build and run a repo with it, how an
operator adds a new repo to qq, and qq in one guide (a 2026-10-06 snapshot). Purpose
and research action live in [GOAL.md](GOAL.md).

It also holds suraj's infrastructure research from his writings: teardowns of the build, CI and release systems qq is modelled on or could use, starting with Chromium's, and notes on training infrastructure such as Mixture of Kittens. Each is published here as a report with a one-pager.

## Research and findings so far

Each finding was checked against the repos at the commits listed in the app's
footer on 2026-10-05.

Every link on the map is backed by a pin or README line in the repo it

### Mixture of Kittens (GPU training infrastructure)

Each finding is suraj's, from his writings at `a5a356d`.

- **When Cursor profiled the training runs behind Composer, the mixture-of-experts layer consumed more than half of end-to-end training time; MoK fuses all of that layer's communication and computation into a single, fully deterministic megakernel.** Source: [Mixture of Kittens](output/report/2026-10-15-mixture-of-kittens.md#the-last-bottleneck), §01. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **MoK pulls on forward dispatch and pushes on forward combine, and the backward pass mirrors them; pull-based dispatch delivers up to 29% higher NVLink utilization when experts are imbalanced, and Cursor measured its initiation at 18 µs against 103 µs push-based.** Source: [Mixture of Kittens](output/report/2026-10-15-mixture-of-kittens.md#push-or-pull), §03. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **With TMA-driven loads and stores, less than a third of the 148 SMs can saturate NVLink bandwidth, so MoK splits the chip into communication SMs and computation SMs that run simultaneously and hand off minibatch by minibatch.** Source: [Mixture of Kittens](output/report/2026-10-15-mixture-of-kittens.md#splitting-the-chip), §05. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **Incoming tokens land in a fixed-size ring buffer of a few hundred megabytes, so MoK drops no tokens, keeps memory bounded and never asks the slow Grace CPUs to size buffers; the ring buffer is the piece that makes the megakernel viable at all.** Source: [Mixture of Kittens](output/report/2026-10-15-mixture-of-kittens.md#ring-token-buffers), §06. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **MoK's open-source release is a working production artifact under Apache-2.0 that requires Blackwell SM100/SM103 hardware (GB200/GB300 NVL72), CUDA 13.0+, Python 3.12+ and PyTorch 2.10+.** Source: [Mixture of Kittens](output/report/2026-10-15-mixture-of-kittens.md#the-megakernel-dividend), §07. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **In Cursor's benchmarks the fused MoK layer reached a peak 2.37× MXFP8 forward-pass speedup over the fastest of four public baselines on one NVL72, and the production run on 512 GPUs went from 760.9 to 1,070.2 tokens/sec/GPU, a 1.41× end-to-end gain; the gap between the two is Amdahl's law.** Source: [Mixture of Kittens](output/report/2026-10-15-mixture-of-kittens.md#the-numbers), §08. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **suraj reads MoK as a sign that the abstraction stack is collapsing, that determinism is becoming a feature, with bitwise-reproducible training close to a requirement for clean on-policy RL, and that the unit of optimization is now the 72-GPU rack.** Source: [Mixture of Kittens](output/report/2026-10-15-mixture-of-kittens.md#why-it-matters), §09. Verified: 2026-10-15.

### Chromium teardowns

Each finding is suraj's, from his writings at `a5a356d`.

- **Chromium's CI is 1,211 builders in 18 buckets, all generated by lucicfg from 125 Starlark files inside the repository they build; nothing is deployed, and a change to the fleet is a CL.** Source: [The tree that configures the fleet](output/report/2026-10-15-chromium-ci-cd-and-releases.md#02-the-tree-configures-the-fleet), §02. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **Of 604 builder entries in the generated `commit-queue.cfg`, 32 are required and can block a landing, and LUCI CV adopts an earlier tryjob with the same `ReuseKey` while it is younger than 24 hours; on one live CL, the full run reused 32 of 34 tryjobs.** Source: [The tree that configures the fleet](output/report/2026-10-15-chromium-ci-cd-and-releases.md#06-the-commit-path-upload-to-tryjob), §06. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **A CQ build fails only on tests that failed with the patch, failed again when their shards were retried, and passed without the patch; three of the four phases exist to distinguish "your change is broken" from "this test is broken", against what Chromium's docs put at roughly 20,000 flaky tests.** Source: [The tree that configures the fleet](output/report/2026-10-15-chromium-ci-cd-and-releases.md#07-inside-a-cq-build), §07. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **After submit, 133 tree closers, twelve gardener rotations and LUCI Bisection keep the tree green; Bisection may create ten reverts a day and submit four, and submits only when the culprit is younger than 21,600 seconds (six hours).** Source: [The tree that configures the fleet](output/report/2026-10-15-chromium-ci-cd-and-releases.md#09-the-waterfall-and-its-humans), §09. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **Per Chromium's release docs, the milestone cadence halved to two weeks with Chrome 153's stable release on 2026-09-08; branch day is a config event, and the same Starlark read with `is_main` false yields 256 builders on the M155 branch against trunk's 1,211.** Source: [The tree that configures the fleet](output/report/2026-10-15-chromium-ci-cd-and-releases.md#10-the-release-cycle-and-the-branches), §10. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **V8's release is fed by a ref, `refs/heads/lkgr`, that a builder moves forward every six minutes, so the release script never has to decide what is good, and a day on which V8 lands nothing produces no release.** Source: [The tree that configures the fleet](output/report/2026-10-15-chromium-ci-cd-and-releases.md#11-the-release-scripts), §11. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **Rollers write about a fifth of chromium/src's `main`: in the week of 2026-09-14 to 2026-09-21, 940 of 4,513 commits (20.8%) came from the two Skia autoroll accounts, and of 3,893 roll commits in four weeks, 3,428 carry a `Tbr:` footer and 14 a `Reviewed-by` (counted 2026-09-22).** Source: [DEPS, gclient and what replaced goma](output/report/2026-10-15-chromium-deps-and-goma.md#08-the-roller-fleet), §08. Verified: 2026-10-15.
- **The declarative file is easy to change and the ecosystem around it is not, so Chromium keeps the old interface alive while the new mechanism carries the load: `git_dependencies = 'SYNC'` is still in `src/DEPS` three years after a submodule stage two scheduled for 2023 Q4, and reclient is still pinned nearly a year past its announced removal.** Source: [DEPS, gclient and what replaced goma](output/report/2026-10-15-chromium-deps-and-goma.md#12-what-this-design-buys-and-what-it-costs), §12. Verified: 2026-10-15.
